# Corridors, gates, professional division — narrative-level design
Permanent capital gates: monumental freestanding stone portals, clear visual passage to other capital-city environment, fluid doorway-like walking transition. ~300m high and ~100m wide is illustrative. Maintained civic infrastructure, not a new mathematical derivation.
**One-anchor leapfrog:** ONE reusable mobile sigil anchor carried ahead by scout (possibly CSR flight), ONE Corridor Card with operator staying with traveling group. Scout places/activates anchor; cardholder discovers active anchor's projected field interface, completes compatibility/topological handshake, opens corridor. Guards can go through first to secure arrival; group, wagons, animals, cargo follow subject to capacity. Card is deactivated, corridor closes, group rests while SAME anchor is moved forward again. No second portable anchor. Sigil-inscribed metal cube fitting a dimensional ring is possible, not mandatory. Range depends on anchor/card/operator/conditions; no fixed values.
Civilian standardized anchors/cards generally interoperable; other compatible users including bandits might interface with active civilian anchors. Does NOT grant automatic control of an existing corridor. Military Architects make restricted anchor sigil topology and military Cardsmiths make matching specialist Corridor Cards, providing access-controlled handshake. Exact encryption/security, simultaneous contention, interruption and discovery OPEN. The field-topology analogy does not license claims of real cryptographic strength.
**Professional separation:** Architects/Runesmiths design/inscribe sigils, gates and anchors; Cardsmiths design/engineer cards; Transductionists operate equipment. Shared mathematics, different products. Earlier claim that Cardsmiths engineer both sigils and cards is SUPERSEDED. Same separation applies to Kaira's workbench: architect-made sigil, cardsmith-operated professional cards.
Roads, wagons, ships, guard patrols and waystations remain important. Corridors move people, not patrol skipped territory. Caravan guards secure anchor sites; bandits may discover anchors but risk confronting hardened adventurers or Crown guards. Keep prose simple, no new math.
